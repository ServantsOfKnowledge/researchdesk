{{ _("About Servants of Knowledge") }}

{{ _("Servants of Knowledge (SOK) is an Indian non-profit that digitises books and makes them open: the printed heritage of India, in its own languages, scanned, processed and shared so that anyone can read, search and cite it.") }}

{{ _("Today SOK runs 15 scanners that it builds itself from open designs, digitising 10–15 lakh pages every month, and builds the open tools that take a book from the scanner to the reader: Repub, which straightens and reads each scan, and Research Desk, the library platform behind this site. A library that cannot staff several systems can run the whole chain on its own.") }}

{{ _("SOK works with libraries and institutions that hold books worth keeping: the Gandhi Bhavan libraries, the National Law School of India University (33,000+ books), the Indian Academy of Sciences, and offline libraries gifted to science centres and trusts. It opens what Indian copyright law allows, and invites authors, families and publishers to open more with Creative Commons licences.") }}

{{ _("The work began in 2015 when two independent digitisation efforts joined, started scanning in 2018, and began building its own scanners when COVID-19 cut off imported ones. In 2024 it was incorporated as Anubhava Mantapa Servants of Knowledge Association, a Section 8 non-profit. The name honours the 12th-century assembly founded by Basava, open to people of every caste and gender, as a model for open access to knowledge. Its motto is “Scanning is the New Spinning”: ordinary daily work that anyone can join, building something everyone owns.") }}
